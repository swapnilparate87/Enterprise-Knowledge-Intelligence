SUMMARY: DRAFT guide describing how Support and Customer Success staff should write, review, publish
and retire Knowledge Base articles using the Problem, Environment, Cause, Resolution, Related
articles template. Not yet in force.

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT - Northstar Systems is a fictional company. This document exists for development,
testing and demonstration only and is not real legal, employment, security or compliance advice.


1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
--------------------

DRAFT NOTICE: this document is a DRAFT (version 0.9) and is not yet in force. The approved current
practice for Knowledge Base articles is whatever the related approved documents say, in particular
CS-002 for the troubleshooting content and CS-007 for how tickets lead to articles. Where this draft
differs from approved practice, approved practice applies until this guide is approved and
published.

The purpose of this draft is to give authors one consistent method for writing Knowledge Base (KB)
articles about the Northstar Nexus Platform, Nexus Connect and Nexus Insight, so customers and
Support Engineers can find a correct answer without opening a ticket. The scope covers article
structure, writing style, the review and publication workflow, review cadence, and archiving. It
applies to Support Engineers, Support Leads, CSMs and Engineering staff who contribute articles. It
does not change ticket triage, priorities or customer commitments.


2. DEFINITIONS
--------------

  - KB article: a customer-readable or internal-readable page that explains one problem and its
    resolution.
  - Author: the person who writes the article.
  - Reviewer: a second person who checks the article before it is published. Two-person review is
    proposed to be mandatory.
  - Status: Draft, Published or Archived.
  - Article owner: the Support Lead responsible for the article after publication.


3. ARTICLE TEMPLATE
-------------------

Every article should use five headings in this order:

  1. Problem: what the customer sees, including the exact error text or code, such as NX-2004.
  2. Environment: the Nexus version, product (Platform, Connect or Insight), and any relevant
     Connector or configuration.
  3. Cause: why the problem occurs, in one or two plain sentences.
  4. Resolution: numbered steps that a customer can follow; one action per step.
  5. Related articles: links to articles on neighbouring problems.

Example: An article for NX-3010 would have the problem as "requests fail with HTTP 429 and code
NX-3010", environment as "any supported Nexus version", cause as "the tenant exceeded the default
limit of 1,000 requests per minute", and resolution as "add backoff, honour the Retry-After header,
then retry".


4. WRITING RULES
----------------

  - Use plain, short sentences. Write in English; a Hindi version may be added on request for India
    customers.
  - Use the product terms exactly: Workspace, Pipeline, Connector and Webhook.
  - State the version affected. Check support dates in CS-006 and never write that a version is
    supported without checking.
  - Do not include customer-specific data such as tenant IDs, customer names, request IDs or
    personal data in any article.
  - Do not include internal system names such as NorthAssist, NorthDesk or NorthWatch in customer-
    facing articles. Internal-only articles may name them.
  - Do not promise delivery dates or future features. Do not describe an unreleased fix.
  - Keep the title searchable: begin with the symptom or code, for example "NX-4003 schema mismatch
    in a Pipeline".
  - Use the documented values: default connector timeout 30 seconds, adjustable up to 120 seconds;
    rate limit 1,000 requests per minute per tenant.
  - Do not include security-sensitive details. Anything about a vulnerability must be agreed with
    the Security Desk first.


5. WORKFLOW
-----------


5.1 Create

An author writes the article in Draft status after a resolved ticket that has been seen at least
twice, or after a P1 or P2 incident that produced a reusable resolution. A ticket-derived article
must have all customer data removed before it is saved.


5.2 Review

A second person, who is not the author, reviews every article before publication. The reviewer
checks that the steps work on the stated version, that the language is clear and neutral, that no
customer data is present, and that links are correct. A change to a security-related article also
needs the Security Desk's agreement.


5.3 Publish

The reviewer moves the article from Draft to Published. The article owner is recorded. Articles for
customer-facing portals are published through NorthAssist.


5.4 Review cycle and archive

Every published article should be reviewed at least every 12 months. At review, the owner checks the
steps against the current supported versions. If the problem no longer exists, or the only affected
version is out of support, the article is moved to Archived. Archived articles are not shown to
customers but are kept for reference. An article must not be deleted unless it contains data that
should not have been stored.


6. QUALITY CHECKS
-----------------

The Head of Support proposes a monthly sample of articles. Each is scored on accuracy, clarity,
template compliance and absence of customer data. Articles scoring poorly are returned to Draft.
Useful measures include how often an article is used to deflect a ticket and the proportion of
tickets closed with a linked article.


7. EXAMPLES OF GOOD AND POOR PRACTICE
-------------------------------------

Good: "Resolution: 1. Open Workspace settings. 2. Select API tokens. 3. Rotate the token. 4. Update
the token in your integration."

Poor: "Contact support to get a new token." This is not a resolution, because the customer can
rotate the token without help.

Good: stating that the timeout can be raised up to 120 seconds after checking endpoint latency.

Poor: stating that the connector will never time out. This is untrue and risks customer trust.


7A. LINKING ARTICLES TO TICKETS
-------------------------------

When a Support Engineer resolves a ticket using an article, the article should be linked to the
ticket in NorthAssist. If no article exists and the same issue has been resolved twice, the engineer
should raise an article request to the Support Lead. If an article is found to be wrong, the
engineer must flag it at once; the article owner should correct or withdraw it within 2 business
days. Articles must never be edited silently after publication: each change goes through the same
second-person review, and the review date is updated so the 12-month cycle restarts.

Example: Three Standard customers report NX-4003 after upgrading a Connector. The Support Lead asks
an engineer to draft an article titled "NX-4003 schema mismatch in a Pipeline after a Connector
update", with the resolution to re-map fields and check the Connector version. A second engineer
reviews it, and it is published.
