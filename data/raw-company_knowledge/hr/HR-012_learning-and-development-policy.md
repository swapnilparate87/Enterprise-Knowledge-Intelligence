**Summary.** Explains the annual learning budget of INR 40,000 per employee in NorthLearn, how to request courses and certifications, when fees are reimbursed, the 12-month retention clawback for programmes above INR 25,000, mandatory training, and how learning links to performance and budgets.

*SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT - Northstar Systems is a fictional company. This document exists for development, testing and demonstration only and is not real legal, employment, security or compliance advice.*

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy explains how Northstar Systems Private Limited supports the learning and professional growth of its employees. It sets the individual learning budget, the way to request and book learning, the rules for certifications and external programmes, mandatory training, and the commitment to stay that applies to expensive programmes. It aims to give every employee a fair and simple route to build skills that help them in their role and career, and to make the company's spend on learning transparent and accountable.

The policy applies to all confirmed employees in Bands B1 to B7 in Bengaluru, Mumbai and remote locations. Employees on probation may use free and internal learning in **NorthLearn**, the learning portal, and may request paid learning with the approval of their manager and the HR Business Partner. Contractors and interns are not covered unless their agreement says so.

## 2. Definitions

- **Learning budget**: the amount each employee may use per fiscal year (1 April to 31 March) for approved learning.
- **Approved learning**: a course, certification, workshop, conference, book or subscription that is relevant to the employee's role, development plan or the company's needs, and approved under this policy.
- **Certification fee**: the fee for an exam or certificate from a recognised provider, such as a cloud, security, project management or data qualification.
- **Retention clawback**: the commitment to repay part of the cost of a programme if the employee leaves within a set period.
- **Learning and Development (L&D) Lead**: the HR role that owns this policy and NorthLearn.
- **Development plan**: the learning actions agreed with the manager, usually at the annual review or mid-year check-in.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Employee | Plans learning with the manager, requests it in NorthLearn, completes it and shares evidence of completion |
| Reporting manager | Approves requests that fit the role and workload, ensures time is available, discusses learning in one-to-ones |
| Department head | Approves requests above INR 25,000 and exceptions to the budget |
| L&D Lead | Runs NorthLearn, negotiates with providers, tracks budget use and mandatory training |
| HR Business Partner | Advises on development plans and checks clawback agreements |
| Payroll and Benefits Manager | Reimburses fees through NorthPay and recovers clawback amounts |
| Finance | Tracks spending against department cost centres, in line with the Budget Planning and Cost Center Policy (OPS-008) |

## 4. The Learning Budget

### 4.1 Amount

Every eligible employee has a learning budget of INR 40,000 per fiscal year. The budget is available in NorthLearn from 1 April and ends on 31 March. It is the same for all Bands. The budget covers the cost of course fees, exam fees, books and approved subscriptions. It does not cover travel, accommodation or meals for a course, which must follow the Travel and Expense Reimbursement Policy (HR-004) and need a separate travel approval in NorthPay.

### 4.2 Unused budget

Unused budget does not carry forward to the next fiscal year and cannot be paid out in cash. Employees who join during the year receive a pro-rata budget based on the number of full months remaining in the fiscal year, rounded up to the next INR 1,000.

### 4.3 What counts

Learning that may be paid from the budget includes:

- Online courses and learning platform subscriptions that appear in the NorthLearn catalogue or are approved by the manager.
- Professional certifications and examination fees.
- Workshops, bootcamps and conferences, with the registration fee only.
- Books and learning materials related to the role.
- Language or communication courses that support the role.

Learning that is not covered includes degree programmes (which need a separate approval by the Chief People Officer), hobby courses, learning with no link to the role or company needs, and subscriptions the employee already holds.

## 5. Requesting Learning

### 5.1 Procedure

1. The employee discusses the learning with the reporting manager and links it to the development plan or a goal.
2. The employee raises a request in NorthLearn with the provider, programme name, dates, total cost, the expected benefit and the time away from work.
3. The reporting manager approves or rejects within 3 working days.
4. If the programme costs more than INR 25,000, the department head must also approve, and the L&D Lead checks the clawback terms.
5. After approval, the employee books the learning. For programmes paid directly by the company, the L&D Lead arranges payment as a purchase through NorthBuy. For other programmes, the employee pays and claims reimbursement.

Requests should be raised at least 10 working days before the start date, or earlier for programmes with early registration deadlines.

### 5.2 Direct payment and reimbursement

The company prefers to pay providers directly for programmes above INR 10,000, using a purchase order in NorthBuy, in line with the Purchase Approval Policy (OPS-001). For lower amounts, the employee may pay and claim reimbursement in NorthPay within 15 calendar days of the payment or completion date, with the invoice and proof of payment. Receipts are mandatory for every expense above INR 500.

### 5.3 Time to learn

Approved learning during working hours counts as work time. Managers should give employees reasonable time to learn, and employees should plan around team commitments and release windows. Learning that takes place outside working hours is voluntary and does not earn compensatory leave.

## 6. Certifications

The fee for a certification is reimbursed after the employee passes. The employee must submit the pass certificate or result and the payment receipt in NorthPay within 15 calendar days of the result. If the employee does not pass, the first exam fee is not reimbursed, but the employee may request approval to use the learning budget for one retake, with the manager's approval and a revised study plan. If the budget is not enough to cover the fee in a given year, the manager and department head may approve an exception, or the employee may take the exam in the following fiscal year.

Where a certification is required by a customer contract or by the company, such as a partner certification for a cloud provider, the department pays for it from its own cost centre, and the cost does not reduce the individual learning budget. Examples are required security, cloud or product certifications for Customer Success and Support staff.

Example: A Support Engineer (Band B2) in Mumbai wants to take a cloud practitioner certification costing INR 8,000. The manager approves in NorthLearn. The engineer pays the fee, passes the exam and submits the certificate and receipt in NorthPay within 15 calendar days. The INR 8,000 is reimbursed with the next eligible payment, and the remaining learning budget for the year is INR 32,000.

## 7. Retention Clawback for Larger Programmes

A programme whose cost is more than INR 25,000 carries a service-retention commitment of 12 months. The commitment starts on the date the programme ends, or the date the company makes the final payment, whichever is later. The employee signs a short learning agreement in NorthLearn before the programme starts. If the employee resigns or is terminated for misconduct within 12 months of the start of the commitment, the employee must repay the company's cost of the programme on a pro-rata basis.

The pro-rata amount is the programme cost multiplied by the number of months remaining in the 12-month period divided by 12. Part-months are rounded in the employee's favour. The amount is recovered from the final settlement, and any balance after F&F must be paid before the employee's last working day, in line with the Compensation, Payroll and Salary Revision Policy (HR-010). The clawback does not apply if the company ends the employment without cause, such as a redundancy, or if the employee leaves because of a medical reason documented by a doctor.

Programmes at or below INR 25,000 carry no retention commitment.

Example: An Engineering Lead (Band B5) attends a leadership programme costing INR 60,000, paid by the company. The commitment runs for 12 months from the end of the programme. The lead resigns after 4 months. Eight months remain, so the clawback is INR 60,000 x 8 / 12 = INR 40,000, recovered from the final settlement.

Programmes should not be split into smaller payments to avoid the INR 25,000 threshold. Doing so is a breach of the Code of Conduct and Ethics (HR-008).

## 8. Mandatory Training

Every employee must complete the mandatory programmes in NorthLearn, which currently include security awareness, the Code of Conduct and Ethics, anti-harassment awareness and data privacy. New joiners must complete security awareness within 14 days of joining; other modules follow the due dates set in NorthLearn. These are free of charge, do not use the learning budget and count as working time. The L&D Lead reports overdue training each month to managers, and repeated failure to complete it is escalated to the department head and HR. Interviewers, people managers and members of the Internal Committee also need role-specific modules.

## 9. Internal Learning

Employees are encouraged to share knowledge. Internal learning includes brown-bag sessions, mentoring, job shadowing, rotation projects, guilds and the NorthLearn library. Time spent as a trainer or mentor on approved programmes is recognised in the performance review described in the Performance Review Process (HR-007). Internal learning is free and does not reduce the learning budget.

## 10. Learning and Performance

Learning actions should be part of the development plan agreed at the April annual review and revisited at the October mid-year check-in. A manager should not refuse a request that clearly supports an agreed development goal unless the budget is exhausted or the timing creates a serious business problem, and should explain the reason in writing. Using the learning budget is not a requirement, and not using it does not affect the rating.

## 11. Exceptions and Budget Top-ups

An employee who needs more than INR 40,000 in a fiscal year for a role-critical programme may ask the manager for a top-up. The department head may approve a top-up from the department learning pool, within the department's budget. Exceptions above INR 1,00,000 in a single request need the CFO's approval, because they count as unbudgeted spend under the Delegation of Authority. The Chief People Officer decides exceptions to the clawback rule.

## 12. Records

L&D records, including requests, learning agreements, certificates and attendance, are kept in NorthLearn. They are part of the employee record and are kept for 7 years after the employee leaves. Employees must not upload confidential company or customer material to external learning platforms.
